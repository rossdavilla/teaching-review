### PSY157 · Personal Relationships — 5

**Contextual predictors of state loneliness: The role of social company, activity, location, and duration of contextual exposure**  
*Journal of Personality and Social Psychology* · Busch et al. · [10.1037/pspp0000619](https://doi.org/10.1037/pspp0000619)  
→ **L7 Friendship**

22,031 day-reconstruction episodes from 2,508 Germans aged 17–95. Being alone predicted state loneliness; people were lonelier at home than at work; TV raised it; self-care and prolonged social contact lowered it.

**Why it matters:** Your slides frame loneliness dispositionally (“often have average # of friends”). This makes it a context-driven state with teachable triggers — and **lonelier at home than at work** is a genuinely counterintuitive opener.

**Which Way Does Cupid’s Arrow Go? Distinguishing Happiness and Meaningfulness Within Romantic Relationships**  
*Journal of Social and Personal Relationships* · Thiruchselvam et al. · [10.1177/02654075261477595](https://doi.org/10.1177/02654075261477595)  
→ **L6 Interdependence**

Two studies (n=426; n=711). Sexual desire and satisfaction and fewer sacrifices predicted relationship happiness but not meaningfulness. Negative self-disclosure predicted meaningfulness only. People preferring meaning over happiness showed higher satisfaction, optimism, closeness and forgiveness.

**Why it matters:** Your Social Exchange slides treat satisfaction as one quantity (Outcomes vs. CL). This splits the outcome in two and shows the costs that **reduce happiness can build meaning** — a direct complication of the rewards/costs ledger.

**Is Authenticity Perceptible? Accuracy in Judgments of State Authenticity From Expressive Behavior**  
*Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin* · Castro et al. · [10.1177/01461672261481957](https://doi.org/10.1177/01461672261481957)  
→ **L4 Social Cognition**

Five studies. Partners accurately read each other’s authenticity after sacrifices and after discussing personal distress — but failed during relationship conflict. Outside observers watching thin-slice videos of those same conflicts succeeded.

**Why it matters:** A clean boundary condition for your “Accuracy of Partner Perceptions” slides, and the thin-slice result links back to the **0.1-second first impression** material earlier in the same lecture.

**Extending Truth-Default Theory to Misinformation: Connecting Misinformation Scholarship and Deception Research**  
*Perspectives on Psychological Science* · Moore & Hancock · [10.1177/17456916261475589](https://doi.org/10.1177/17456916261475589)  
→ **L10 Stresses · L11 Persuasion** · also PSY170

Connects truth-default theory to misinformation scholarship: TDT predicts findings the misinformation literature found surprising, and its analytic approaches apply to detection tasks and intervention evaluation.

**Why it matters:** You already teach truth bias and the finance-lie study where 0% detected deception. This **names the theory** organizing those findings and bridges to material students care about.

**Self-Esteem as a Shared Resource Beyond Romantic Relationships: An Extension Into Friendships and Parental Relationships**  
*Journal of Social and Personal Relationships* · Hoplock et al. · [10.1177/02654075261498961](https://doi.org/10.1177/02654075261498961)  
→ **L7 Friendship · L4**

308 dyads; Actor–Partner Interdependence analyses extend prior romantic self-esteem findings to close friends and primary caregivers.

**Why it matters:** Your partner-feedback material (self-verification vs. self-enhancement) is romantic-only. This **licenses extending it to friendship**, where Lecture 7 already lives.

### PSY170 · Industrial/Organizational Psychology — 5

**A meta-analytic examination of the criterion-related validity and cognitive loading associated with traditional and new situational judgment test paradigms**  
*Journal of Applied Psychology* · Lievens et al. · [10.1037/apl0001420](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0001420)  
→ **L2 Selection**

457 independent samples, 302 studies, N=244,257. SJTs predicted job performance, academic performance, OCB and counterproductive work behavior; validity was higher in real applicant settings. Multimedia SJTs matched text-based validity at lower cognitive load; webcam responses had the lowest load of all.

**Why it matters:** Your selection lecture covers biodata, work samples, references, background checks, cybervetting and interviews — **but not SJTs**, now among the best-evidenced tools available. Your closing slide asks how interviews can be improved; this is the missing third answer, with the largest N in the batch.

**Industrial-organizational science that matters: Social impact and policy implications in the Journal of Applied Psychology**  
*Journal of Applied Psychology* · Casper et al. · [10.1037/apl0001425](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0001425)  
→ **L1 Intro · L13 Future**

BERTopic modeling of 5,865 empirical JAP articles from 1917 to June 2024: 99 topics, 25 themes. Trends 1980–2024 show growth in health and well-being, flat DEI publication, an inverted-U for justice and legal topics, and the disappearance of labor-related research.

**Why it matters:** A far better “History of I/O” slide than Yerkes and WW1 testing alone — a data-driven century of the field’s own priorities. The **vanishing of labor research** also speaks to your Lecture 5 claim about pensions and union power.

**Daily job crafting with generative artificial intelligence: Implications for work meaningfulness and engagement**  
*Journal of Vocational Behavior* · Güven et al. · [10.1016/j.jvb.2026.104297](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvb.2026.104297)  
→ **L13 Future · L4 Motivation**

Ten-day daily diary, 149 UK employees using Gen-AI daily. Four crafting dimensions linked to engagement via need satisfaction then meaningfulness; the competence pathway was most consistently supported, relatedness least. Employees framed automating hindering demands as reallocating cognitive resources toward valued work, not cutting workload.

**Why it matters:** Your AI lecture rests on E0/E1/E2 exposure and EPOCH, both conceptual. This is **empirical evidence about what employees actually do with AI**, and it maps onto the Job Characteristics Model you already teach.

**Are the basic psychological needs of self-determination theory necessary for employee well-being?**  
*Journal of Vocational Behavior* · Ding et al. · [10.1016/j.jvb.2026.104308](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jvb.2026.104308)  
→ **L4 Motivation**

Uses Necessary Condition Analysis because the 30-year necessity claim had never been tested with a method matching necessity logic. Across three studies autonomy, competence and relatedness were confirmed as necessary for job satisfaction, work meaningfulness, positive affect and engagement. Power and status were not.

**Why it matters:** This **strengthens** your SDT slide rather than undermining it, and gives a sharp methods aside: “necessary” and “correlated on average” are different claims needing different statistics.

**A meta-analysis of the dynamism of emergent states and team processes**  
*Journal of Applied Psychology* · Loignon et al. · [10.1037/apl0001407](https://doi.org/10.1037/apl0001407)  
→ **L8 Groups**

Over 3,000 correlations from longitudinal studies, roughly 11,000 teams. Team conflict showed the most inertia, affect the least; team performance formed consistent feedback loops with later states and processes.

**Why it matters:** Your teamwork material is static — problems and fixes. **“Conflict is the stickiest thing in a team”** is memorable and actionable next to your social-loafing remedies.

### PSY175 · Human Sexuality — 6

**Sex Education in the Age of Artificial Intelligence: A Qualitative Analysis of ChatGPT-3.5 Responses to Adolescents’ Questions on Romantic Relationships and Sexuality**  
*Archives of Sexual Behavior* · Couture et al. · [10.1007/s10508-026-03575-2](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-026-03575-2)  
→ **L10 Sex Education · L1**

393 real anonymized emails from 14–16-year-olds sent to a youth support organization, submitted to ChatGPT-3.5. 17.8% were flagged as potentially violating the content policy. Responses that came through normalized the experience, validated emotions and promoted help-seeking.

**Why it matters:** Your lectures argue “porn is becoming the new sex education.” The sequel is already here. The **17.8% refusal rate** is the number that lands: the tool is least available exactly where teens most need it.

**Generative AI vs. Human Sources for Sexual Health Information: Credibility, Trust, and Behavioral Intentions in a U.S. College Sample**  
*Archives of Sexual Behavior* · Woerner et al. · [10.1007/s10508-026-03594-z](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-026-03594-z)  
→ **L10 Sex Education**

N=266 college students randomly assigned to believe identical sexual-health passages came from genAI, an academic expert, a community health professional or a peer influencer. The genAI-attributed version scored lower on accuracy, clarity, warmth, trustworthiness, self-efficacy and willingness to act — largest gaps for pleasure and safety content.

**Why it matters:** A clean source-credibility experiment on your own student population, rhyming with the source-credibility material in PSY170 Lecture 11. Teachable conclusion: **AI works best paired with a human**.

**Use of Post-Coital Strategies to Prevent Pregnancy: Findings from a National Sample of Adolescents and Young Adults in the United States**  
*The Journal of Sex Research* · Steiner et al. · [10.1080/00224499.2026.2736663](https://doi.org/10.1080/00224499.2026.2736663)  
→ **L5 Fertility · L1** · also PSY157

Nationally representative 2025 survey, n=1,259 people assigned female at birth aged 15–29. Of 633 who had had penile-vaginal sex, 25% used a post-coital strategy at last sex; only 8.9% used an effective one; 16.1% used a non-evidenced strategy, most commonly praying (9.4%). Contraceptive knowledge was negatively associated with use.

**Why it matters:** Your Lecture 1 opens with a sexual-illiteracy poll. This is a 2025 nationally representative replacement, and **“9.4% prayed”** will land harder than any percentage currently on the slide.

**Problematic Pornography Use Among Swedish Adolescents: Characteristics and Clinical Implications**  
*Archives of Sexual Behavior* · Piwowar et al. · [10.1007/s10508-026-03544-9](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-026-03544-9)  
→ **L9 Sexual Behavior** · also PSY157

Representative sample of 3,106 Swedish final-year high school students. Self-perceived problematic use 10.4% overall — 18.8% of boys vs 3.9% of girls — associated with more time spent, exposure to violent content, abuse history, mental health problems, risky behavior and non-suicidal self-injury.

**Why it matters:** Your slide says “1 in 3 teens believe porn has negatively influenced them,” which is a belief measure. This gives **measured prevalence, a large gender split and a correlate profile** from a representative sample.

**“It’s Like You’re Trying to Defuse a Bomb”: Fear, Responsibility, and Shame in Young Men’s Sexual Lives**  
*Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy* · Forrest et al. · [10.1080/0092623x.2026.2740624](https://doi.org/10.1080/0092623x.2026.2740624)  
→ **L9 Consent · L12**

Thematic analysis of 31 in-depth interviews with heterosexual men aged 18–32 who came of age in the “age of consent.” The authors’ most striking finding was how rarely these men described enjoying sex. Themes: anxiety and vigilance, moral concern about harming a partner, and shame arising from self-criticism as much as accusation.

**Why it matters:** An uncomfortable, high-quality counterpoint about what consent education can feel like from the inside. **Excellent discussion material for a mixed-gender classroom** precisely because it complicates the standard framing.

**Facial Asymmetry and Sexuality in Women**  
*Archives of Sexual Behavior* · Jaguszewska et al. · [10.1007/s10508-026-03527-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10508-026-03527-w)  
→ **L8 Symmetry · L3 Attraction** · also PSY157

Weak negative correlations between facial asymmetry and recent partner count (ρ=−.23), sociosexual attitude (ρ=−.25) and desire (ρ=−.21) — all non-significant once place of residence (rural vs urban) was controlled (.42 < p < .91).

**Why it matters:** The most directly **corrective** item this month. Your symmetry slides state that symmetry signals health and drives mate selection; this shows how fast such correlates evaporate under one confound. It doubles as a research-methods moment for both courses.

> ⚠️ **Note.** The correlations did not survive a single confound control. The teachable point is the fragility, not an effect.
